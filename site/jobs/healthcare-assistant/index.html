The role

What does an agency healthcare assistant do?

As an HCA you give people the daily care that keeps them safe, clean and comfortable. Each shift is different, but most include:

  • Personal care: washing, dressing and continence care
  • Helping people eat and drink, and recording what they have
  • Moving and positioning people safely, including with hoists
  • Taking basic observations like temperature and blood pressure
  • Spotting changes in health or mood and telling the nurse in charge
  • Keeping clear and accurate care notes
  • Spending time with people and treating them with dignity

What you need

  • The right to work in the UK
  • Care experience, ideally 6 months or more in a UK setting
  • Good spoken and written English
  • Willingness to complete mandatory training (we provide it free)
  • A kind, calm and reliable approach

Questions

HCA job questions.

What is the difference between an HCA and a support worker?

HCAs usually work in care homes and hospitals, with more personal and clinical care. Support workers often help people live in their own homes and community, with more focus on daily living skills. Many people do both. See our support worker jobs.

Do I need a qualification to be a healthcare assistant?

You do not need a degree. Most services want care experience and up-to-date mandatory training. A Care Certificate or NVQ helps, but it is not always needed.

How soon can I start?

Once your checks are complete. For most people this takes one to two weeks. Having your documents ready speeds it up.

Do you offer healthcare assistant jobs with visa sponsorship?

Sponsorship depends on the current Skilled Worker rules and our openings. Many HCA roles do not meet the current salary and skill rules. Ask us before you apply, and never pay anyone for a sponsorship offer.
