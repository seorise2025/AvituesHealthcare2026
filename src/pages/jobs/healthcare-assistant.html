What does an agency healthcare assistant do?

You give people the everyday care that keeps them safe, comfortable and treated with dignity. Most shifts include:

  • Washing, dressing and continence care
  • Helping people eat and drink, and recording it
  • Moving and positioning people safely, including with hoists
  • Basic observations such as temperature and blood pressure
  • Noticing changes in health or mood and telling the nurse in charge
  • Writing clear care notes

What you need

  • The right to work in the UK
  • Paid care experience. Most shifts need at least 6 months in a UK care setting.
  • Good spoken and written English
  • Up-to-date mandatory training, or willingness to complete it
  • A calm, kind and reliable approach

How pay works

Each shift has an hourly rate. It depends on the type of setting, the time of day, the day of the week and the location. You see the rate before you accept a shift, and it is never below the National Minimum Wage.

Questions

Healthcare assistant questions.

What is the difference between an HCA and a support worker?

Healthcare assistants usually work in care homes and hospitals with more personal care. Support workers often help people live in their own homes and community, with more focus on daily living skills. Many people do both. See support worker work.

Do I need a qualification?

You do not need a degree. Services look for care experience and current mandatory training. A Care Certificate or a level 2 or 3 care qualification helps.

Is visa sponsorship available for HCA work?

Sponsorship depends on the current Home Office rules for the role and the salary. Ask us before you apply. Never pay anyone who offers you a sponsored care job.
