What the job involves

You give people the everyday care that keeps them safe and comfortable, and you do it in a way that respects their dignity. On a typical shift you might:

  • help people wash, dress and use the toilet
  • help with meals and drinks, and record what they've had
  • move and reposition people safely, including with hoists
  • take basic observations such as temperature and blood pressure
  • notice changes in someone's health or mood and tell the nurse in charge
  • write clear care notes

What you'll need

  • The right to work in the UK
  • Paid care experience, usually at least six months in a UK care setting
  • Good spoken and written English
  • Up-to-date mandatory training, or willingness to do it

How pay works

Each shift has its own hourly rate, depending on the setting, the time of day, the day of the week and where it is. You'll see the rate before you accept, and it is never below the National Minimum Wage.

HCA questions

What's the difference between an HCA and a support worker?

HCAs usually work in care homes and hospitals, with more personal care. Support workers often help people living in their own homes, with more focus on everyday life. Plenty of people do both. See support worker roles.

Do I need a qualification?

You don't need a degree. Services look for care experience and current mandatory training. A Care Certificate or a level 2 or 3 care qualification helps.

Is visa sponsorship available?

No. We don't hold a sponsor licence, so you need the right to work in the UK before you apply. Never pay anyone who offers you a sponsored care job.
