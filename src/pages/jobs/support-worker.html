What does a support worker do?

  • Help with cooking, cleaning, shopping and managing money
  • Support people to get out: college, work, appointments, friends
  • Give personal care where it is part of the support plan
  • Support with medication, following the service's policy
  • Follow support plans and behaviour support plans
  • Keep clear daily notes

What you need

  • The right to work in the UK
  • Paid care or support experience
  • Patience, good judgement and respect for people's choices
  • A driving licence helps for community support, but is not always needed

What is a sleep-in?

You stay overnight and can sleep, but you are there if someone needs you. Sleep-ins are usually paid differently from waking hours. Your consultant will explain the rate before you accept.

Questions

Support worker questions.

Do I need qualifications to be a support worker?

You do not need a degree. Experience, current mandatory training and the right attitude matter most.

Will I work alone?

Some supported living shifts are lone working. We only offer those to workers with enough experience, and we tell you before you accept.

Support worker or healthcare assistant?

Many people do both. See healthcare assistant work.

Working in Wales?

Most social care workers in Wales must register with Social Care Wales within six months of starting. Ask us about it when you register.
