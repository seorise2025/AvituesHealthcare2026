What the job involves

  • Helping with cooking, cleaning, shopping and money
  • Supporting people to get out: college, work, appointments, seeing friends
  • Personal care, where it's part of the support plan
  • Medication support, following the service's policy
  • Following support plans and behaviour support plans
  • Writing daily notes

What you'll need

  • The right to work in the UK
  • Paid care or support experience
  • Patience, good judgement, and respect for people's own choices
  • A driving licence helps for community support, but isn't always needed

Sleep-ins

On a sleep-in you stay overnight and can sleep, but you're there if someone needs you. Sleep-ins are usually paid differently from waking hours, and your consultant will explain the rate before you accept.

Working in Wales or Northern Ireland

Most social care workers in Wales must register with Social Care Wales within six months of starting. In Northern Ireland, social care workers must be registered with the Northern Ireland Social Care Council (NISCC). Ask us about it when you register.

Working with us

  • We tell you if a shift is lone working before you accept
  • Weekly pay

Support worker questions

Do I need qualifications?

You don't need a degree. Experience, up-to-date mandatory training and the right attitude matter most.

Will I work on my own?

Some supported living shifts are lone working. We only offer those to people with enough experience, and we tell you before you accept.

Support worker or HCA?

Plenty of people do both. See healthcare assistant roles.
