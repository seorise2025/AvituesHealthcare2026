Support workers for supported living services

In supported living, one unfamiliar face can unsettle someone for days. We supply support workers, senior support workers and RNLD nurses for learning disability and autism services, and we work hard to send the same people back.

Routine matters

Tell us who the person gets on with, what upsets them and how their day usually runs. We use it when choosing who to send.

Lone working

Plenty of shifts have no colleague on site. For those, we look for experienced, confident support workers.

Out in the community

Shopping, appointments, college, days out. Tell us if the worker needs to drive, and give us notice, because fewer can.

What we cover

  • Support workers, senior support workers, team leaders and RNLD nurses
  • Day shifts, waking nights and sleep-ins
  • One-to-one and two-to-one support hours
  • Block bookings to keep the same workers

What we don't

  • Writing or reviewing support plans
  • Any restrictive practice that isn't in the person's plan

Our support workers are trained in positive behaviour support, autism awareness, epilepsy awareness and safeguarding adults.

Cost and timing

Sleep-ins, waking nights and day support are charged differently. Higher needs and two-to-one support need more experienced people, which affects both the rate and how much notice we need.

We agree all of this in writing before the first shift.

Supported living questions

Do you cover sleep-ins?

Yes. Tell us the hours and any waking duties so we can agree the right rate and brief the worker properly.

Can the person meet the worker first?

If there's time, yes. Book ahead and we'll arrange an introduction before the first shift.

What do you need to know about the person?

Only what the worker needs to support them safely: needs, routines and risks. Please share it with us securely rather than through the website form.

Need support workers?

Send the hours and we'll come back to you.
