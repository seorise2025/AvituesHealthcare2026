Support workers who respect the routine.

Who it is for

For providers supporting people to live their own lives.

Routine and familiar faces

Tell us who the person gets on with and what upsets them. We use it when we choose who to send.

Lone working

Many shifts have no colleague on site. We look for confident, experienced support workers for these.

Community support

Shopping, appointments, college and days out. Tell us if the worker needs to drive.

Included

  • Day shifts, waking nights and sleep-ins
  • One-to-one and two-to-one support hours
  • Block bookings to keep the same workers
  • Requests for drivers, where available

Not included

  • Writing or reviewing support plans
  • Restrictive practice not set out in the person's plan
  • Workers starting before their checks are complete

Training our support workers hold

  • Positive behaviour support
  • Autism awareness
  • Epilepsy awareness
  • Safeguarding adults
How it works

Booking support workers.

  1. Tell us about the person

    Needs, routines, likes and anything to avoid. Only what the worker needs to know.

  2. Agree hours and rates

    Including how sleep-ins are paid.

  3. We match workers

    On experience, skills, and driving if needed.

  4. Keep them coming back

    Ask for the workers who fit. We book them first where they are free.

Questions

Supported living questions.

Do you cover sleep-in shifts?

Yes. Tell us the sleep-in hours and any waking duties so we can agree the right rate and brief the worker.

Can the person we support meet the worker first?

Where time allows, yes. Book ahead and we can arrange an introduction before the first shift.

What do you need to know about the person?

Only what the worker needs to support them safely: needs, routines and risks. Please share it securely, not in the online form.
